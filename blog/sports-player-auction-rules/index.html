How to Create Rules for a Sports Player Auction

16 October 2026

A sports player auction needs written rules for eight things: who is eligible, how players are categorised, each team's purse, squad size limits, base prices, how much each bid must rise, what happens to unsold players, and how disputes are settled. Publish them to every team owner before auction day, so the room is applying rules, not inventing them.

Use the checklist below to write your own.

Who is eligible to register?

State who can enter the player pool: age limits, location, affiliation (company, school, club), and whether a registration fee applies. Say who approves registrations and by when. A player with a pending payment is not yet an approved player; say so.

How are players categorised?

List the categories and what puts a player in each, for example by role or level. If squads need a minimum number from a category, write the number.

What is each team's purse?

State the purse per team, whether all teams get the same amount, and any deductions (for example, for retained players).

What are the squad size limits?

Give the minimum and maximum players per team. Add any reserve rule, such as "a team must always keep enough purse to fill its remaining slots at base price."

How are base prices set?

Say whether base prices are per category or per player, and that they are fixed before the auction.

How much must each bid increase by?

Define the bid increment: the minimum amount a new bid must exceed the current bid. Some leagues use a single step; others use bigger steps once the price passes a threshold. Choose what suits your purse sizes and publish it.

How is the next player chosen?

State whether the operator picks the order, follows a published list or category order, or uses a random draw. A visible random draw, such as spin-to-select, removes arguments about favouritism.

How does a sale close?

Explain the countdown: how long it runs and when the player is declared sold. State that the decision of the operator, as shown on the main screen, is final.

What happens to unsold players?

Decide whether unsold players return in a later round, at the same or a lower base price, or are left out. Whatever you choose, publish it before the day.

How are disputes and mistakes handled?

Name one person (not a team owner) who decides disputes. State what happens if a bid is placed by mistake. Confirmation before a bid is sent helps, but your rulebook should still say whether a confirmed bid can be withdrawn.

A short rules template

  1. Eligibility and registration deadline
  2. Categories and squad composition
  3. Purse per team and any deductions
  4. Squad minimum and maximum, plus reserve rule
  5. Base prices
  6. Bid increments
  7. Player order and selection method
  8. Countdown and close of sale
  9. Unsold players
  10. Disputes and final authority

Frequently asked questions

What rules does a player auction need?

Eligibility, categories, purse per team, squad size, base prices, bid increments, player order, close of sale, unsold players and dispute handling.

When should auction rules be shared?

Before auction day, ideally before registration closes, so owners and players know the rules in advance.

What is a bid increment?

The minimum amount by which a new bid must exceed the current bid. The league sets it in its rules.

Who settles disputes during an auction?

A named neutral person, usually the organiser or operator, whose decision is final. Write this into the rules.
