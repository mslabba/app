Sponsors on the Auction Screen, Not on a Side Banner

7 November 2026 · By PowerAuction Team

Sponsors want to be seen. At a sports player auction, the live auction screen is a natural place for that visibility: it shows the player, bidding activity, and countdown that people follow throughout the event.

A side banner still has its place. But if you’re arranging sponsorship for a league auction, consider screen branding alongside physical signage. This post covers how PowerAuction shows sponsors with the player information and team logos, and how to prepare those logos for the event.

Why does the auction screen matter for sponsors?

A sports player auction brings people together to follow team selection. Screen branding puts sponsors alongside that activity:

  • In the room. Spectators facing the big screen can see sponsor logos alongside the auction information.
  • For team owners. Owners can follow the shared screen while bidding from a phone or laptop in the venue.
  • On the stream. If your YouTube auction stream includes the auction screen, it can carry that branding to online viewers too.

A physical banner can work well in a camera shot or at the entrance. Screen logos offer another placement, particularly when the broadcast captures the auction display directly. Neither guarantees attention; the layout, camera framing, and readability matter.

What belongs on the live auction screen?

PowerAuction’s big screen shows:

  • The player’s image, name, stats, and category
  • The PowerAuction logo, plus the auction and event name at the top
  • Team logos
  • Sponsor logos

The player information helps owners follow the auction, while sponsor logos appear on the same display. When a new bid comes in, the countdown resets — one minute by default.

Prepare sponsors during event setup, alongside assigning owners and allocating purses. That gives you time to check the display before the first player is called.

How should organisers prepare sponsor logos?

The source files affect how clearly logos appear. Before auction day:

  1. Ask for high-resolution files. Request a transparent PNG suitable for display. Keep a vector original if the sponsor provides one, so a designer can export the required format. Check accepted upload formats before preparing the final files.
  2. Check contrast. A dark logo against a dark background can disappear. Ask for light and dark versions where available, then check the version you’ll actually use on the display.
  3. Keep every logo readable. Agree the sponsor list early and check how the complete set appears together. Don’t promise a particular placement or size before verifying what the display supports.
  4. Check from the back of the room. Stand where the furthest spectator will be. If the logos are hard to recognise, review the files and display setup before doors open.
  5. Check the stream framing. If a camera is filming the screen, confirm that the logos are inside the shot. If you’re capturing the screen directly, check the broadcast preview for cropping or overlays.

Give one person responsibility for collecting the files and checking them on the actual equipment. A clean source file alone doesn’t guarantee a clear projector image.

When can sponsors be visible?

Check visibility across the event rather than assuming one preview represents the whole auction:

  • During bidding. Confirm that logos remain readable while the player and bid information are displayed.
  • Between players. Check the transition and selection screens before promising continuous visibility.
  • In the replay. If the recording captures the branded display, those appearances can remain visible after the event.

State what you will provide in the sponsor agreement: screen logos, physical signage, announcements, or other agreed placements. Keep those commitments separate from the auction rules, which explain how bidding and team selection work.

Avoid promising audience reach, viewing time, or commercial results unless you have evidence to support those promises.

What can you show sponsors after the auction?

Keep screenshots or photographs that show the sponsor logos on the actual display. If you streamed the event, save relevant recording timestamps. These are direct evidence of the placement you delivered.

PowerAuction also lets organisers review sold players, team spend, and category distribution, and export reports. Every bid and sale is kept on record, so the organiser can look back if a result is questioned. Squads can also be shared after the auction.

Those records describe the event’s activity, not sponsor impressions or attendance. If a sponsor asks how many people watched, use separately verified venue or streaming figures. Share only information you’re authorised to disclose; a sponsor doesn’t automatically need detailed bidding or spending records.

A quick sponsor-branding checklist

Before the first player is called:

  • Agree the sponsor list and collect suitable logo files.
  • Check the accepted upload format and prepare the final versions.
  • Add sponsors during event setup.
  • Check readability on the actual big screen, including from the back of the room.
  • Check what appears in the stream and recording.
  • Make sure the sponsor agreement matches what you can deliver.
  • Assign someone to capture evidence of the agreed placements.

Frequently asked questions

Where should sponsor logos appear at a player auction?

The live auction screen is a useful placement alongside physical signage. Check readability for the room and camera framing for online viewers before promising visibility.

Can PowerAuction show sponsor logos on the auction screen?

Yes. The big screen shows sponsor logos alongside player information, team logos, and event branding. Prepare sponsors during event setup, before bidding starts.

How many sponsor logos should I put on the screen?

Use a set that stays readable on the actual display. Verify the supported layout before agreeing sponsor placements or sizes; don’t assume unlimited logos will fit clearly.

Do sponsors benefit if we stream the auction?

A stream can extend the visibility of screen branding if the logos are included and readable in the broadcast. Check the preview and recording rather than assuming everything visible in the room will appear online.
