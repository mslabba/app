How to Organize a Sports Player Auction (Without a Spreadsheet)

15 October 2026

To organize a sports player auction, work in this order: open player registration, approve players and place them in categories, create teams with owners and purses, set base prices and squad rules, publish the rulebook, then run the live auction one player at a time. Most auction-day problems come from skipping a step before the day, not from the bidding itself.

New to auctions? Start with What Is a Sports Player Auction?. Here is the plan, step by step, including where spreadsheets usually break.

Step 1: How do you collect player registrations?

Publish one registration form and share the link everywhere players are: WhatsApp, email, social media, or a direct URL. Ask for what you need to categorise players: name, contact, playing role and the details your league uses. If you charge a registration fee, collect it in the same flow through a payment gateway, so each player shows as paid or pending. Then approve or reject entries.

Spreadsheet trap: form responses in one sheet, payment screenshots in a chat, approvals in someone's head. One list avoids three versions of the truth.

Step 2: How should players be categorised?

Group players by role or level, whatever suits your sport and league. Categories let you set base prices per group and write squad rules like "each team needs at least two players from Category A."

Step 3: How do you set up teams and owners?

Create each team, assign its owner, upload the logo, and set the purse.

Step 4: What base prices and rules do you need?

Decide base prices per category, squad minimum and maximum, how much each bid must go up by, and what happens to players nobody bids on. Write the rules down and share them with owners before the day.

Step 5: What do you need on auction day?

  • A venue or call with a big screen everyone can see.
  • One operator running the console: choosing or spinning the next player, showing the player card, running the countdown.
  • Each team owner on their own device with access to their team dashboard.
  • Sponsor logos ready if sponsors expect screen visibility.
  • A rehearsal with a few test players.

Step 6: How do you run the live bidding?

Bring up one player at a time with the base price shown. Owners bid; the countdown runs; the highest bid wins when it ends. Update the purse and squad immediately and show the change before moving on. For a cricket-specific walkthrough, see How to Conduct a Cricket Player Auction.

Step 7: What do you do after the auction?

Share final squads with every team and review the numbers: purse allocated versus spent, highest and average prices, team spend. Keep the record; it settles disputes later.

Frequently asked questions

What is the first step to organize a player auction?

Open player registration with a shareable form, then approve players and place them in categories.

How many people do I need to run an auction?

At minimum, one operator to run the live console and one owner (or representative) per team. Larger events add a host and someone to manage screens.

Can I collect registration fees?

Yes. A fee is optional; in PowerAuction it is collected through a payment gateway during registration and each player shows as paid or pending.

Can I organize an auction for sports other than cricket?

Yes. The same steps apply to football, futsal, basketball, volleyball, corporate and school or college events.

Do I need a spreadsheet?

Not if your registration, teams, purses and live bidding are in one system. Spreadsheets tend to split into several versions on auction day.
